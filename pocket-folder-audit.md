# Coach Movers Pocket Folder — Audit & Strategy

Use case reframed: this is what an **estimator hands the homeowner** during an in-home free estimate. So the folder has four jobs to do — and we should design against each.

| Moment | Who's looking | What the folder must do |
|---|---|---|
| **Arrival** | Homeowner sees folder on the counter as the estimator walks in | Signal "professional outfit, not a guy with a truck" |
| **Walkthrough** | Estimator opens it, points at sections | Give the estimator a script — process, what's included, why us |
| **Leave-behind** | Folder sits on the kitchen table for 2-7 days | Hold the estimate paperwork, stay top-of-mind vs. 3-5 competing quotes |
| **Decision** | Homeowner picks one folder to actually call | Make the *call action* dead simple and one detail memorable |

Right now we have one good first-impression front cover (Variation A) and one good reference document interior (Variation B). They're being compared as alternatives — but they should be **combined**, because the front cover and the inside have different jobs.

---

## What's strong right now

- **Front cover (Variation A) is the right hero.** B&W mover-on-suburban-front-steps photo + lime accent + "Moving Made Easy." reads as *Apple/IKEA, not Craigslist*. This earns the first 3 seconds.
- **Step-by-step process (both variations).** "Book → Plan → Pack/Move → Settle in" is the right narrative for a hesitant homeowner who's never moved before. Keep this.
- **Spine works as a counter-top tab.** When 5 folders sit on a kitchen table, the spine is what they see. Both variations get this right.
- **Trust badges + reviews.** Standard, but necessary table-stakes.

## What's weak — by panel

### Front cover

- "Moving Made Easy." is a *tagline*, not a *promise*. Every mover says they're easy. The cover should say something a competitor can't copy in five minutes. Suggestions:
  - **"23 years. 12,000 moves. Zero broken promises."** — earns trust with a number
  - **"Quoted flat. Charged flat. Period."** — owns one specific differentiator
  - **"Long Island & Connecticut's most-recommended mover, since 2002."** — geographic ownership
- The phone number is bottom-right, small. **The phone number should be the second-largest thing on the cover** — this folder is going to be sitting next to four others. Make it impossible to miss when the homeowner reaches for it.

### Back cover

- The big quote ("Twenty-three years of moves...") is **brand-voice marketing copy** — fine — but it's repeating things the front cover already says. Burn this real estate on something differentiating:
  - **A map** of NY/CT showing service area + headquarters pin (Melville)
  - **Pricing transparency table** ("Studio apt local: starts $XYZ • 2BR local: starts $XYZ • Long-distance: $XYZ/mile")
  - **A guarantee** ("If we miss our quoted arrival window by more than 30 minutes, your first hour is free")
- Reviews — currently anonymized initials + town. Stronger: **full names + photo + Google star ratings + count**. "Sarah K." reads like a fake testimonial; "★★★★★ Sarah Kowalski, Huntington — verified Google review (1 of 847)" reads like proof.
- **Trust badges are vague.** "Licensed & Insured" is meaningless without a license number. NYS DOT #, USDOT #, MC #. These are searchable; printing them is a flex that says "we're not hiding."

### Spine

- "Coach Movers · Licensed & Insured" is wasted space. When the folder is on the kitchen counter, the spine is the **action prompt**. Suggestion:
  - **"COACH MOVERS · CALL (800) 917-4446"** — phone reads as the main thing
  - Or **"COACH MOVERS · COACHMOVERS.COM/QUOTE"** — drives them to the quoted-online flow

### Inside left ("What you get")

- 6 generic features. Half of these (Licensed & Insured, Professional Crews) duplicate the badges. **Cut to 4 real differentiators**, each with a number:
  - **"Flat-rate pricing — what we quote is what you pay"** (anti-hourly-billing pitch)
  - **"Climate-controlled storage — 12,000 sq ft in Melville"**
  - **"Same-week scheduling — most moves booked within 5 days"**
  - **"Background-checked crews — every mover, every job"**
- Move "what you get" from a feature list to **a comparison table**: "Coach Movers vs. the cheap quote vs. the franchise." Three columns, ten rows. Estimators love this — it gives them something to point at while saying "now look at row 4."

### Inside right ("Your move, step by step")

- 4 steps is right. But step 1 is "Book a Free Estimate" — which has *already happened* by the time the homeowner has this folder. **The first step on the leave-behind should be the next action**: "Decide & lock your date" or "Sign your quote." Re-anchor the timeline to the customer's actual position in the funnel.

### Pocket flap

- "Let's Move." is good but should sit next to the *contents* of the pocket — a one-line index: "Inside this folder: your estimate, our COI, packing checklist, references." Tell the homeowner what you handed them, so they don't lose pieces.

### What's missing entirely

1. **Day-of checklist** ("Confirm with crew lead 48hr before • Disconnect appliances morning-of • Have payment ready"). Reduces customer anxiety, reduces inbound support calls. Belongs on the inside-left bottom or as a tear-out card.
2. **Damage / claims policy in plain English.** "If we break it, here's exactly what happens." This is the single biggest worry homeowners don't say out loud.
3. **What's NOT included** ("We don't move pianos, pets, or hazardous materials. Here's a contractor we recommend for pianos."). Honesty is a differentiator; competitors hide this.
4. **Owner / founder note + signature.** Family business since 2002 is a story — tell it in 4 sentences with a real signature on the back. Massive trust signal, costs nothing.
5. **A QR code to a 30-second video walkthrough** of the warehouse / a recent move. Cheap to produce, and "they have a video" is something homeowners mention to spouses.

## Strategic recommendation — pick one direction

**Don't ship Variation A or Variation B as-is.** Ship a hybrid:

- **Outside** = Variation A (Editorial Hero). The photograph + bold tagline + lime is what wins the first impression. Keep the dark front cover, keep the lime divider, keep the white "Coach Movers" mark, but rewrite the headline to *one specific promise* (see suggestions above).
- **Inside** = Variation B (Architectural Clean). Once the folder is open, you need *information density*, not mood. Ink-on-white, numbered lists, a comparison table, a guarantee — that's what gets read while the homeowner is comparing quotes on Tuesday night.
- **Spine + back cover** = also Variation B's restraint. The spine is a wayfinding label; the back cover should be *useful reference* (map, license #s, plain-English claims policy), not a second mood shot.

This split mirrors how the folder actually gets used: cover sells, interior informs.

## Concrete next steps (in priority order)

1. **Rewrite the headline.** "Moving Made Easy." → one specific, defensible promise. Block 30 minutes with the founder; this is a copy decision, not a design decision.
2. **Print the license numbers.** NYS DOT, USDOT, MC. These should be findable on the back cover and in the spine fine-print.
3. **Replace the back-cover quote with a service-area map + guarantee.** Both are differentiators competitors won't copy.
4. **Cut "Inside left" from 6 features to 4 numbers.** "Climate-controlled storage" is dead text; "12,000 sq ft of climate-controlled storage in Melville" is a flex.
5. **Add a pocket-contents index line.** "Inside: your estimate, COI, packing checklist."
6. **Generate the comparison table.** Coach vs. cheap quote vs. franchise. Estimator's favorite tool.
7. **Get a real founder signature.** Scan, drop on back cover with a 4-sentence note.
